Abstract
7
Automated Market Makers (AMMs) are the main liquidity providers in the Decentralised
8
Finance (DeFi) space. They are programmes that follow some coded rules to determine and
9
adjustpricesandvolumes. Amongstthoserules,themostcommononeistheconstant-product
10
formula, which is followed by the most important AMMs in DeFi: Uniswap in the Ethereum
11
blockchain [Unia] and Raydium in the Solana blockchain [Raya]. In this paper we explain the
12
mathematical formulas that are used to compute prices and volumes in AMMs with constant-
13
productformulas,andutilisethisframeworktostudytheeffectofpriceandvolumechangeson
14
AMMsfromtwopointsofview: (1)theliquidityprovider’s(i.e. theAMM)and(2)thetrader’s
15

16

17

18
loss)oftheAMM.Wealsoanalysetheeffectofliquiditybalance/imbalanceintheAMMonthe
19

20

22

23

1 Automated Market Makers (AMMs) and traditional mar-
49
ket makers (MMs)
50
Automated Market Makers (AMMs) play the role of liquidity providers in protocols or projects in
51
theDecentralisedFinance(DeFi)space. AMMsareprogrammes,hencetheyoperatewithouthuman
52
intervention, following a number of rules that govern the way these AMMs provide liquidity and
53
adjust prices.
54
Atraditionalmarketmaker(MM)buysandsellsanasset,providingliquiditytopotentialbuyers
55
or sellers. The MM will quote two prices: the ask price at which they are willing to sell, and the
56
bid price at which they are willing to buy. The difference between these two prices is know as the
57
(bid-ask) spread; this spread is the remuneration of the MM for providing liquidity: they sell at a
58
higher price and buy at a lower price.
59
When you trade with an automated Market Maker (AMM), instead of buying and selling one
60
token you enter a swap i.e. you exchange one token for another token. This sounds different than
61
the MM, but it is not really: when you buy an asset to an MM you pay them with a currency, say
62
USD; when you sell the asset you provide the latter to the MM in exchange of a certain amount of
63
USD. Therefore, if we see the asset as token X and the USD currency as token Y then MMs and
64
AMM are actually the same.
65
2
Electronic copy available at: https://ssrn.com/abstract=4053924

The key concept of spread for an MM is translated as slippage cost for AMMs. Indeed, just like
66
the spread helps the MM to avoid adverse selection (in a nutshell, to be on the wrong side of the
67
trade when prices have a trend), the slippage helps the AMM to keep in balance the two assets in
68
its liquidity pool. More precisely, the slippage in the AMM can be thought as the half spread in
69
the MM: in both cases these two quantities are the extra cost relative to the market price (current
70
swap value for the AMM, current mid price for the MM) that the trader incurs when dealing with
71
an AMM or a MM.

2.3 Duality between prices and token amounts
125
There is a “principle of duality” in Equation (2), in the sense that we can either assume that the
126
prices P (t) and P (t) are given and then compute the token amounts x(t) and y(t), or either
127 X Y
assume that the token amounts are given and compute the implied prices that satisfy the balance
128
condition (2). Let us elaborate this point in detail.
129
On the one hand we have a traditional MM. As we assumed in Subsection 2.2, the MM will
130
have the two prices P (t) and P (t) as exogenous or independent variables, and then they will
131 X Y
optimally adjust their liquidity provision x(t) and y(t) to satisfy the two constraints, namely the
132
constant-product formula (1) and the constant-notional formula or balanced liquidity provision (2).
133
On the other hand, we have an AMM like Uniswap in the Ethereum blockchain [Unia]. If the
134
prices P (t) and P (t) were coming from an external source then data risk on the price feed that is
135 X Y
used,butalsoacoderiskontheactualprogramwithintheAMM(knownas“smartcontract”)that
136
computes the optimal liquidity distribution x(t) and y(t). In order to eliminate these two sources of
137
risk, Uniswap uses what we call the “principle of duality”: instead of having the prices P (t) and
138 X
P (t) as independent variables and the token amounts x(t) and y(t) as dependent variables (like
139 Y
the traditional MM does), Uniswap (or any other AMM) uses the token amounts as independent
140
variables and compute the prices endogenously as a function of these token amounts.
141
Inthelightofthis“principleofduality”betweenpricesP (t)andP (t)andtokenamountsx(t)
142 X Y
and y(t), we can either consider (1) that the prices move first (based on the chosen price feed) and
143
then the token amounts have to be optimally adjusted by the MM, or (2) that the token amounts
144
move first (due to swaps arriving at the AMM or changes in its liquidity provision) and then the
145
endogenous prices are optimally adjusted. Regardless of whether we take the point of view of the
146
MM or the AMM, in both cases the end result is that the balance condition (2) holds.
147
In our subsequent analysis and examples, we will assume the point of view of a MM: just like
148
ourselves, we assume that the reader is familiar with basic economic and financial concepts like
149
utility functions, budget lines, market making and liquidity provision. Therefore, we will assume
150
that price changes occur first and then and compute the corresponding token amounts. However,
151
thereadercantakethepointofviewoftheAMMwithoutanylossofgenerality: inthisframework,
152
the formulas we will present are reverse-engineering the token amounts (which occurred first) from
153
their impact in the observable prices (which occurred second).

7 Conclusions
330
1. AMMs are close relatives of MMs: if we see the security as a first token and the currency paid
331
for said security as a second token, dealing with an MM becomes a swap in the AMM sense.
332
2. The slippage a trader incurs when dealing with AMMs can be interpreted as the half spread
333
in MMs: both are deviations from the current market price due to the trade. As such, they
334
can be considered as a proxy for market impact and depth of liquidity within an AMM.
335
19
Electronic copy available at: https://ssrn.com/abstract=4053924

3. Undertheeffectofapricemove,theimpermanentlossinpercentageisinvarianttotheavailable
336
liquidity: nomatterhowbigistheavailableliquidity,AMMswillalwayslosethesameamount
337
in percentage points when prices move. This is very similar to the “adverse selection” that
338
traditional MMs suffer when providing liquidity.
339
4. The impermanent loss due to price moves grows faster than linear.
340
5. AMMs become more efficient as their liquidity increases: the slippage decrease and converges
341
to zero.
342
6. Having a balanced pool in notional terms is crucial for price discovery and to maintain the
343
current price levels. If the pool is imbalanced then, compared to the balanced case, the swap
344
costforthetraderwillbehigher(resp. lower)thanexpectediftheirtransactionamplify(resp.
345
decrease) the current imbalance.
346
7. The price differences due to liquidity imbalances can be quite dramatic: due to the constant-
347
productformula,thepricesmoveawayfromtheirequilibriumfollowingapowerlawdynamics,
348
which is much faster than linear.
349
8. The impermanent loss disappears when there is price reversion: all state variables return to
350
their initial values, hence it is as if “nothing had happened”.
351
9. However,ifwewithdrawsomeliquiditybeforethepricereversioniscompleted,wecouldrealise
352
some of the impermanent loss, effectively rendering it “permanent”.
