NYSE specialists
 Designated market makers: specialists (NYSE)
 They are dedicated market makers on a particular security
 They are obliged to quote buy and ask prices all the time
 Based on inventory and risk appetite, they change their quotes
 Positive inventory  need to sell  aggressive ask prices
 Negative inventory  need to buy  aggressive bid prices
 Increased risk (market or liquidity)  increased spread
newsok.com

Self-organised liquidity
 In electronic markets, there are several liquidity providers:
 Traders providing liquidity in the market
 Limit Orders in the Limit Order Book
 And several liquidity consumers:
 Traders consuming the liquidity of the market
 Market Orders
 Traders can change from providers to consumers and vice-versa
 No dedicated dealers or specialists
geek-whisperers.com
 How can electronic markets operate without dedicated market makers?
 It is the aggregate of liquidity providers that makes the market
 But providers (generally) have no obligation to place both ask and bid quotes
 Self-organised liquidity
 Any trader can choose, at any instant of time, to either provide or consume liquidity

Limit Order Book (LOB)
 The LOB is the current liquidity of the market at every
batstrading.com
available price level
 It is the aggregate of all Limit Orders (LOs)
 A LOB has 2 sides:
 Bid side: Prices at which the market buys from clients
 Ask side: Prices at which the market sells to clients
 In electronic markets, dealers are anonymous and real-
time data is aggregated:
 All you see are the total volumes at every price
 But you do not know how many shares each trader
inserted in the LOB
 For regulation, stock markets have the ID of every trader
and every order

Microstructure of the LOB
 Most common priority is price/time priority:
 First, price priority: LOs at the best price are selected first
 Second, time priority: Within the best price level, the oldest LOs are selected first
 But there is also pro-rata priority:
 First, price priority as before
 Second, volume priority: trades are distributed in proportion of the volumes of the LOs
 Tick:
 The smallest price increment in the LO
 Real prices are discrete, not continuous
 Spread:
 Difference between the best ask price and the best bid price
 Price of liquidity: the expected gain of a dealer
 Measure of liquidity:
 Highly liquid markets  spread = 1 tick
batstrading.com
HFT - Mauricio Labadie

Limit orders vs market orders
 When you send a buy market order:
 You buy at the best selling market price, i.e. the lowest available
 Your order hits the ask side: buy MO ↔ sell LO
 If you trade via Limit Orders:
 You have a better price: you earn the spread
 But delayed and uncertain execution: deal-too.com
 Your LO sits passively on the LOB, waiting to be matched against an MO
 Market risk and execution risk
 If you trade via Market Orders:
 You have a worse price: you pay the spread
 But immediate and certain execution:
 Your MO hits the LOB and is matched vs the LOs at the best price
 No market risk and no execution risk

Definition of high frequency trading
 Algorithmic trading firms
 Using computer algorithms to trade
 Which automatically determine individual parameters of the order
 Time, price, size
 With limited or no human intervention
 An automated system makes decisions on initiation, routing and execution of the order
 High frequency trading
 Subset of algorithmic trading
 Infrastructure intended to minimise latency
 No human intervention in order initiation, routing or execution
 High message intraday rate (orders, quotes, cancellations)
 Average of 2 messages per second in a single instrument
 Or 4 messages per second in all instruments

Iceberg
 Goal: Minimise the market impact of a fully visible big limit order
 An iceberg order of (say) 1000 shares consists of two parts:
 A visible part of (say) 100 shares
 A hidden part of (say) 900 shares
 Mechanism:
 Place the visible part in the LOB at the current best price
 Send a first clip of 100 shares
 When the visible part is executed:
 Send a second clip of 100 shares at the same price
 When the second clip is executed:
 Send a third LO of 100 shares at the same price
 We repeat the iceberg game until full execution www.express.co.uk
 In our example, until the 10th clip of 100 shares is executed
 Risk: Incomplete execution if the price moves away from us

Stop Loss & Take Profit
 Stop Loss:
 Set a maximal loss for our portfolio
 Set a minimal price or lower bound for our
portfolio
 When the portfolio value hits the threshold
o Get out of the position: sell everything via MO
 Take Profit:
 Set a maximal gain for our portfolio
 Set a maximal price or upper bound for our
portfolio
 When the portfolio value hits the threshold
 Get out of the position: sell everything via LO
 The LO can be placed in advance e.g. at entry
 How to determine the thresholds?
 Investor’s risk appetite and expected return
 Volatility of the asset

Time-weighted average price (TWAP)
 Goal: Minimise the market impact by spreading a big trade along the day
 Benchmark: Average price of the day
M.C. Escher
 Example: Average 1-minute mid price
 Mechanism: Assume we want to buy 1,000,000 shares
 TWAP splits the full trade evenly in time
 During 8.5 hours: from 8:00 to 16:30 (London)
 1,000,000 / 8.5 ~ 117,000 shares per hour
 1,000,000 / (8.5 * 60) ~ 1,960 shares per minute
 It is a static algorithm:
 The schedule is known a priori
 The times and quantities of the “child orders” are constant
 Risks:
 Very predictable pattern
 Can easily be spotted by HFTs and be “gamed”
 We can add some randomness in the number of shares and the times of the child orders

 Mechanism:
 VWAP splits the full trade based on the intraday historical volume curve
 High historical volume  bigger child order than TWAP
Katsushika Hokusai
 EU open, EU close, US open
 Low historical volume  smaller child order than TWAP
 It is a static algorithm:
 The schedule is known a priori
 But sizes are not constant as in TWAP
 Risks:
 Still predictable pattern (but less than TWAP)
 The historical volume curve can diverge from the real volume curve
 Volume shocks, news, etc

Percentage of Volume (PoV)
 Goal: Execute a big order based on a given fraction of the traded volume in the market
 Benchmark: Market VWAP of the execution interval Eugene Delacroix
 Mechanism: Assume we want to buy 1,000,000 shares
 PoV aims a constant participation of (say) 20%
 10,000 shares traded in the market  PoV executed 2,000 shares
 100,000 shares traded in the market  PoV executed 20,000 shares
 It is a dynamic algorithm:
 The schedule and the end time are not known a priori
 The algorithm depends on the real volume curve, not the historical volume curve
 Risk:
 Very sensitive to volume shocks
 We can add some price protection when there are predictable volume shocks
 Quarterly results, weekly data release, etc
